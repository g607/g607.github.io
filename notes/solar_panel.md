## Key Conclusions

- Current electricity usage is estimated at **~1,463 kWh/year**, annualised from 10 months of OVO data.
- The solar system is expected to generate **2,920 kWh/year**, almost double current household consumption.
- A future EV driving **4,000 miles/year** would consume approximately **1,000 kWh/year**.
- Even after adding the EV, the system should still export around **460 kWh/year**.
- Solar-only payback is estimated at **~12.1 years**.
- Adding an EV improves solar payback to approximately **9.8 years**.
- Replacing the current petrol car saves around **£942/year** in fuel costs.
- Combined solar + EV ownership is estimated to improve household finances by approximately **£1,615/year** versus today.
- The EV substantially improves the economics because self-consumed solar electricity is worth **26.88p/kWh**, while exported electricity earns only **12p/kWh**.
- Panels are expected to last 25+ years, meaning a significant period of net savings after payback has been reached.

## Personal Verdict

For a **£6,570** installed cost including a battery, this appears to be a strong proposition if:

- You can secure a **12p/kWh or better SEG export tariff**.
- You expect to remain in the property for at least 10 years.
- You intend to switch to an EV in the future.
- Electricity prices remain at or above current levels.

At your current usage, the system appears reasonable. With a future EV driving **4,000 miles/year**, the economics become significantly more attractive, reducing estimated payback to **under 10 years** while cutting annual motoring and energy costs by roughly **£1,600/year** combined.
